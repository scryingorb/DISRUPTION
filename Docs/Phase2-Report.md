## Exact DSP designs

In these formulas x is the driven input, d is smoothed DAMAGE/100 in [0,1], and tanh/atan are standard continuous functions. Individual transfer entrypoints also sanitize non-finite input, apply the existing emergency +/-1,000,000 bound, and validate DAMAGE.

### BURN

One biased tanh curve, not stacked nonlinearities:

```
t = 1.4 - 0.65*d
b = 0.18*d*d
z = tanh(b)
y = t * (tanh(x/t + b) - z) / (1 - z*z)
```

At DAMAGE=0 the curve is odd, with headroom t=1.4 and gentle saturation at ordinary levels. Increasing DAMAGE reduces headroom to 0.75 and adds modest asymmetry through bias, progressively increasing density/compression and even harmonics. Subtracting z preserves y(0)=0; dividing by 1-z*z maintains a unity small-signal slope despite bias. There is no hard-clipping stage in BURN. At maximum DAMAGE its asymptotes are approximately +0.637/-0.913 before the global DC blocker. It remains a smooth saturation curve at all settings.

### BREAK

Symmetric, continuously variable C1 clipping knee:

```
t = 1.1 - 0.42*d
w = t * (0.8*(1-d)^2 + 0.01)
a = abs(x)
start = t - w
end = t + w

if a <= start: magnitude = a
else if a >= end: magnitude = t
else:
    u = a - start
    magnitude = start + u - u*u/(4*w)
y = copysign(magnitude, x)
```

The broad low-DAMAGE knee produces soft clipping when driven. Increasing DAMAGE both lowers the plateau and narrows the knee, reaching approximately hard clipping at +/-0.68, with a narrow 0.0068 knee half-width. The knee joins the linear and flat regions with matching value and first derivative; no threshold/algorithm switching occurs. The flattening is the intended distortion algorithm, not a safety limiter. Quiet signals below the knee stay linear even at high DAMAGE.

### RIP

Different positive and negative transfer families, joined with matching small-signal slope and curvature at zero:

```
tPositive = 1.3 - 0.95*d
tNegative = 1.25 - 0.25*d
if x >= 0:
    shaped = tPositive * tanh(x/tPositive)
else:
    shaped = tNegative * (2/pi) * atan((pi/2)*x/tNegative)
y = 1.12 * shaped
```

The positive tanh shoulder is rapid, while the negative arctangent shoulder is longer. Low DAMAGE has mild asymmetry; high DAMAGE produces a much lower positive plateau (+0.392) than negative plateau (-1.12). Threshold imbalance and unlike shoulders produce deterministic even-order distortion, with no noise/randomness in the engine. RIP has a dedicated DC blocker immediately after its nonlinear transfer and before mode mixing/global MIX.

These choices aim for smooth BURN, flattened BREAK and torn asymmetric RIP. The measurable personalities differ, but human listening is still needed to judge musical usefulness. No claim of listening-based signoff is made.

## DRIVE, DAMAGE and level management

DRIVE remains the existing smoothed 0..36 dB input gain. DAMAGE changes headroom, knee width and/or asymmetry inside the selected curve; it is not multiplied into DRIVE. BURN and BREAK retain unity small-signal gain across DAMAGE, while RIP retains its fixed 1.12 slope. Tests verify this invariant and the changing bias/knee/asymmetry, as well as output differences between DRIVE/DAMAGE combinations and between every pair of modes.

BURN's bias-slope normalization avoids gain changing simply because the bias changes. BREAK has no makeup gain. RIP uses a fixed 1.12 (+0.984 dB) factor to offset the lower average level from its asymmetric shoulders. No input-dependent normalization, loudness detector, automatic gain chasing or brick-wall limiter was added. Thresholds were selected to keep natural saturation levels reasonably comparable.

Across 54 combinations of sine/multisine sources, amplitudes 0.1/0.5/1.0, DRIVE 0/12/36 and DAMAGE 0/50/100, the largest steady-state RMS spread across the three real modes was 2.1784 dB. This is an RMS comparison, not a perceptual loudness certification.

CRUSH and CHAOS intentionally preserve Phase 1 identity processing, including driven gain. At high DRIVE these placeholders can be substantially louder than the implemented modes. Their levels are not representative of their future engines; use BURN/BREAK/RIP for comparative listening and keep OUTPUT conservative when exploring the placeholder modes.

## DC protection and smoothing

RIP's per-channel first-order DC blocker uses double-precision history:

```
r = exp(-2*pi*5/sampleRate)
g = (1+r)/2
filtered[n] = g*(input[n]-input[n-1]) + r*filtered[n-1]
```

The corner is 5 Hz at every sample rate, with unity gain at Nyquist. Histories are independent for mono/stereo, reset during prepare/reset, and values below 1e-24 are flushed to zero. It prevents RIP's asymmetric mean from leaking into the dry/wet stage. RIP is processed even when its mode weight is zero, keeping its DC history current with DRIVE/DAMAGE and avoiding a stale-state burst when selected.

The existing final 5 Hz DC blocker remains. RIP therefore has two DC blockers at fully wet settings, giving approximately 0.24 dB total attenuation at 30 Hz. For other modes the final blocker removes DC caused by input or BURN's modest asymmetry. A new internal 20 ms dcBlend fades the final correction off/on when targeting fully dry/nonzero MIX; this fixes the old abrupt m>0 endpoint gate without sacrificing full DC removal at settled partial wet settings. Settled MIX=0 remains exactly dry before OUTPUT, and bypass returns sanitized clean input. DC history continues running during dry/bypass.

Smoothing durations, measured in wall-clock time at each sample rate:

- DRIVE, OUTPUT, MIX, bypass: existing 20 ms linear amplitude ramps.
- DAMAGE: 10 ms ramp, with curve coefficients derived from that smoothed value for each sample.
- Mode weights: 5 ms linear, complementary fades over the fixed five-engine array. New selections retarget current weights, so rapid mid-fade changes remain a convex blend.
- Final DC correction engagement: 20 ms internal fade; no new host parameter.

The weights are advanced once per frame, shared across channels; no buffer/engine object is allocated on switches. Stateless BURN/BREAK and placeholders are only evaluated when weighted in; RIP stays warm. No latency is introduced (0 samples reported). MIX and bypass use convex interpolation to avoid cancellation of small processed audio against extremely overloaded dry input.

## Aliasing and future oversampling

All engines currently run at native sample rate. Smooth BURN still generates above-Nyquist harmonics; BREAK's flattened peaks generate stronger high-order content; RIP adds both even and odd harmonics. These fold into the audible band. HQ remains inactive, and aliasing has not been solved.

An integer-cycle developer measurement drives a sine at 0.21*sampleRate with amplitude 4 and DAMAGE=100. Its third harmonic at 0.63*sampleRate folds to 0.37*sampleRate. Measured folded-component amplitudes relative to the fundamental are approximately 0.295 BURN, 0.320 BREAK and 0.267 RIP. These bins can include other folded harmonics; they are fold-back evidence, not isolated-third-harmonic estimates. This deliberately extreme test illustrates the current limitation; real-source artifacts depend on frequency, level and controls. See Phase2/aliasing-evidence.csv.

No temporary oversampling system was introduced. The reserved stage before selected-mode processing remains. Future oversampling can wrap the nonlinear mode section, and must prepare RIP's filter at its actual processing rate if that filter runs inside the oversampled section. Native-rate gain, mix, output and bypass smoothing remain separate from that future stage.

Open Phase2/Transfer-Curves.html in a browser for an offline interactive viewer of measured transfer curves at DAMAGE=0/25/50/75/100. Full-resolution numerical data is in transfer-curves.csv. Regenerate the viewer with `python Tools/inspect-transfer-curves.py` after running tests; no Python dependencies are required. It adds no plugin runtime/UI overhead.

That matching gain exists only in the developer files, not the plugin. Raw levels and offline gains are recorded in listening-levels.csv. These synthesized sources are evaluation aids; listening with actual vocals, drums, bass and 808s remains important.

Before considering Phase 3, evaluate the three personalities by ear, especially high-frequency/high-DRIVE material where aliasing is strongest; review maximum RIP asymmetry and low-bass response; and remember the two placeholder modes retain amplified identity behavior.
